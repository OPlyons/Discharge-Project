An optional conversation with someone who has their own experience of mental health difficulties and recovery.

What is a peer support call?

A peer worker uses their own experience and training to support other people. They listen to what leaving hospital has been like for you. Your experience can be very different from theirs.

You might want to talk through a worry, find something to do locally, or simply have someone listen. You decide what matters to you.

Recovery means building a life that feels right for you. A peer worker can help you explore what gives you hope, choice and control.

What might we talk about?

You can read the questions in advance and answer as many as you like.

  • How has it been since you left hospital?
  • What helped you get ready to leave?
  • What was difficult or could have been better?
  • Are your next steps clear?
  • Do you know who to contact if you need help?
  • Is there any support you would like to explore?
  • What would you like the ward to know?
Other prompts you might hear

How has the change in routine or surroundings been for you?

Is there anything you have felt worried about sharing?

Did this call help? What could make it better?

A time and way that suit you

Ask the ward team or discharge coordinator whether your service offers peer support calls. There is no online application.

Tell the team when it is safe to call and what makes it easier to talk. You can ask for an interpreter, simpler words, breaks, another way to communicate or someone you trust to join you.

You decide who joins you

A partner, family member or friend who gives unpaid support can have a conversation about their own experience. You can also have your call on your own.

Staff can listen to a carer’s concerns while keeping your private care information confidential.

Agreeing what happens next

You and the caller agree any next steps. They can share details of local support or pass a request to your care team. The relevant service decides on referrals.

The caller explains what goes into your care record. You choose separately whether an anonymous summary of your feedback can help improve support. If someone’s safety is at risk, the caller may need to tell someone who can help and will explain this where possible.

You may be offered another call in about three months. You can say no or change your mind. Needing hospital care again is not a failure.

Your choices and information

You can say no, ask for a different time, skip a question or stop. Agreeing to a call and sharing feedback are separate choices.

Staff will explain what they record about you, where they keep it and who can see it. They can listen to your concerns while protecting the other person’s private care information.

    How the project works

    Four steps from the ward offering a call to agreeing what happens next.

    The ward offers a callArranging the callTalking togetherAgreeing next steps

    The ward offers a call

    A member of the ward team explains the project. You decide if you want a peer support call after you leave hospital.

    If you say no, the team stops arranging the call. Your usual care continues.

    Next topic

    The call is arranged

    The ward records your choice and a safe way to contact you. The Lived Experience Coordinator arranges a caller.

    Let the team know the best time or if you would like communication support.

    You talk with a peer worker

    The caller checks you still want to talk. You can choose what to discuss, skip a question or end the call.

    If someone needs help to stay safe, the caller follows the agreed route to get help straight away.

    The caller can share information or pass a request to your care team. You decide together what would help.

    Your information and privacy

    You can ask the caller what they write down and who can see it.

    Your choicesWhere notes goPublic summariesUsing the websitesCommunity Navigator

    Your call and your feedback

    You choose whether to have a call. You can skip questions or end the conversation. Giving feedback and agreeing to later contact are separate choices. Saying no does not affect your care.

    The caller explains the notes they are taking. They complete the call and feedback in one Word form, away from the website. Nobody enters your call answers here.

    If someone’s safety is at risk, the caller may need to share information with people who can help. They will explain this where possible.

    Next topic

    Who can see your notes?

    For a person leaving hospital, the caller saves the completed form in their NHS care record on SystmOne. Carers’ notes use their own agreed recording and access arrangements. You can ask where yours will go.

    Agreed project feedback is kept in approved NHS storage. Access is for authorised staff who need it for their work. The completed form stays off this website.

    BackNext topic

    What can be shared publicly?

    An authorised reviewer takes only the feedback you have agreed to share, removes identifying details and checks the summary. A public quote needs separate permission.

    Public updates describe themes. Individual forms, contact details and care records are kept private. The team keeps draft files and figures in its separate working area.

    Feedback and updates
    BackNext topic

    When you use this website

    You can read the pages without entering personal information. Staff can choose a borough and team for a blank form. Those choices stay in the open page. Downloading a form does not send it to the project team. Downloaded files remain on your device when you close the page.

    The host may keep technical records of visits. You can use this website without signing in. It sends technical fault reports to Sentry. These exclude call details, search text and session recordings. Links to other organisations follow their own privacy arrangements.

    If you have questions about your information, the peer support team can help you find the right CNWL team.

    Reading options let you enlarge the text, hide photographs and reduce the colours. Each section also has a written title.

    Read on paper or in Word

    The project leaflet is a PDF. Staff Word forms have large headings, expanding white answer boxes and tick boxes. Complete the form in Word and save it in the agreed NHS record.

    You can print the current page from Reading options. Topic pages print all their topics together.

    The project is working towards WCAG 2.2 AA. Checks with people who use the site and a full accessibility audit are still needed.

    Words about leaving hospital

    Choose a word to read its meaning.

    Discharge

    Discharge means leaving hospital.

    Transition

    A change from one place or kind of support to another.

    Peer support worker

    Someone with their own experience of mental health difficulties and recovery. They use this experience and training to support others.

    Recovery

    Building a life that feels right for you. This looks different for each person.

    Consent

    Choosing to agree after understanding what is being offered.

    Carer

    Someone who gives unpaid support to another person.

    Feedback, care and team roles

    Choose a word to read its meaning.

    Feedback

    What you tell us about your experience.

    Theme

    A main point from people’s feedback.

    Clinical follow-up

    Contact from the responsible care team after you leave hospital.

    Project coordinator

    The person who brings together the project’s feedback and figures.

    Discharge coordinator

    The person who supports the ward to offer calls, attach completed A1 forms to individual SystmOne records and securely send the names list to the LX Coordinator.

    LX Coordinator

    The Lived Experience Coordinator receives the names list securely, checks each person’s SystmOne attachment and uses Part B (B1) to arrange and coordinate the calls.
