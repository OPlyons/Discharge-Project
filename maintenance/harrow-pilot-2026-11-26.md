## Decisions still to agree locally

The source and email do not establish an approved maximum attempt count or closure process. The draft therefore labels the following as proposals: the usual three attempts in agreed periods, exceptions/rearrangements, coordinator ownership of administrative closure, and an acknowledged handover back before closure. The proposed handover records dates, outcomes, concerns, outstanding actions, the named recipient and next action. A backup route is needed when there is no response. Confirm the receiving route and timing with the responsible clinical lead and review usability with Rebecca and ward colleagues. For carers, confirm the appropriate service for their own needs rather than automatically using the other person's clinical team.

No answer does not confirm safety. Follow the agreed plan after each attempt and act earlier on concerns. The clinical team decides further clinical follow-up. Closing a peer-support episode does not end clinical care.

## Source check and clinical boundary

Checked on 26 November 2026:

- [DHSC and NHS England discharge guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/discharge-from-mental-health-inpatient-settings/discharge-from-mental-health-inpatient-settings) identifies clinical follow-up within 72 hours of discharge, with earlier contact where indicated. The local first-peer-attempt aim and weekday calling periods must not replace or delay that clinical follow-up.
- [NHS confidentiality guidance, rule 2](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/looking-after-information/data-security-and-information-governance/codes-of-practice-for-handling-information-in-health-and-care/a-guide-to-confidentiality-in-health-and-social-care/a-guide-to-confidentiality/rule-2) supports necessary, proportionate information sharing. A carer's own call is not permission to disclose the other person's private care information.
